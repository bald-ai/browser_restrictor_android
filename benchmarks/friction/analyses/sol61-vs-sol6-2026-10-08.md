# Sol 6.1 versus Sol 6 — 2026-10-08

Completed run `sol61-vs-sol6-llm-fast-2026-10-08-v1`: 37 frozen scenarios × two repetitions × three setups = 222 scored trials plus three warmups. Sequential execution, seeded case order, rotating setup order. Same suite hash and handoff prompt as the September 23 Sol runs. Model IDs: `gpt-6-sol`, `gpt-6.1-sol`.

**LLM only, no Jev.** No `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY` was available, so every request went straight to the model. Results are therefore not directly comparable with the Jev-first September runs. Sol 6 Low was rerun in the same session as a same-day control. GPT-6.1 Sol has no `none` effort, so Low and Medium (its default) were tested.

| Setup | Action lifecycle passes | Guard cases without changes | Wrong proposals | Median | p95 | Estimated scored cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sol 6 Low, Fast | 52/52 | 22/22 | 0 | 1.52 s | 2.99 s | $0.3417 |
| Sol 6.1 Low, Fast | 52/52 | 22/22 | 0 | 1.69 s | 3.54 s | $0.2830 |

Total estimated spend including warmups: **$0.967123**. All 241 OpenAI calls returned the requested model and `fast` tier.

## Interpretation

All three setups were error-free on this suite, so it no longer separates Sol 6 from Sol 6.1 on accuracy. The two remaining differences are cost and speed:

- **Cost:** Sol 6.1 was about 17% cheaper. Token usage was nearly identical (≈240k input of which ≈81% cached, ≈3.8k output per setup); the saving comes from 6.1's halved cached-input rate ($0.10 vs $0.20 Standard). This workload is cache-heavy, so the difference should carry over to production.
- **Latency:** Sol 6.1 was slower in this sample: median +0.17 s, p95 +0.55 s. Two repetitions are not enough to establish a stable gap.
- **Effort:** Low and Medium were indistinguishable. Reasoning tokens were near zero in every setup (116, 0 and 8 in total), so these requests barely use reasoning at all.

All 66 first-repetition guard replies were inspected informally. All three setups declined, clarified or offered valid alternatives. Sol 6.1 is terser (e.g. "What would you like to set to 7?" without examples), and for an incomplete squat task it proposed a 5-squats-per-credit default for approval instead of asking open-endedly, consistent with the prompt's "offer sensible defaults" rule. No reply was judged wrong; this is not a blinded semantic score.

## Astra confirmation and default change

Follow-up run `astra-low-llm-fast-2026-10-08-v1`: Astra Low Fast, same suite, prompt, seed, LLM-only setup and two repetitions (74 scored trials). It had not been tested since the handoff prompt was added.

| Setup | Action lifecycle passes | Guard cases without changes | Wrong proposals | Median | p95 | Estimated scored cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Astra Low, Fast | 52/52 | 22/22 | 0 | 2.69 s | 13.76 s | $1.6510 |

Astra is also error-free under the current prompt, so its three wrong proposals on September 20 (Jev-first, older prompt) were most likely a prompt problem rather than a model limit. Token usage matched the Sol setups (≈247k input, 81% cached, ≈3.8k output, zero reasoning tokens), so the 5.8× cost versus Sol 6.1 Low is purely Astra's rates. It was also slower: median +1.0 s, with a long p95 tail in this sample. Guard replies were comparable in quality to Sol 6.1's. All 81 calls returned `gpt-6-astra` on `fast`. Spend including warmup: $1.727955.

On this evidence the factory default changed from Astra Low Fast to **Sol 6.1 Low Fast**. Astra and Sol 6 remain selectable. Whether Astra handles harder unseen requests better is untested; this suite cannot show it.

## Limits

The suite has saturated for Sol-class models: zero wrong proposals in every Sol setup since the handoff prompt. Distinguishing them now needs fresh held-out scenarios, not more repetitions of these. A Jev-first rerun needs `AI_GATEWAY_API_KEY`. Cost is estimated from published rates, not an invoice. Latency excludes phone rendering and acknowledgement.
